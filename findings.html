Findings

The census's six headline findings, in the order of the measurement layers, followed by the scorecard for the three expectations recorded in the preregistration. Every quantity describes the configuration of published research relative to modelled burden estimates; none ranks diseases by clinical importance.

1. The pool grew fivefold and rotated towards oncology

The 14-area pool grew from 24,251 publications in 1995–99 to 112,267 in 2020–24. The five head-and-neck cancers jointly rose from 30.7% to 38.5% of the pool, driven by oral cancer (5.1% → 10.3%) and thyroid cancer (11.0% → 14.5%), with other pharyngeal cancer next (4.1% → 5.5%); nasopharyngeal cancer was nearly flat (5.8% → 6.4%). Laryngeal cancer moved against its own group (4.7% → 1.9%): the rotation is within oncology as much as towards it.

2. Otitis media shrank in share, not in output

Its pool share fell from 9.2% to 3.3% while its five-year output rose from 2,229 to 3,706 publications — a textbook denominator effect, in which a still-growing area is diluted by faster-growing neighbours.

3. Hearing loss is the central level finding

In 2020–24 hearing loss carried 72.7% of the seven-cause DALY pool against 31.6% of the same pool's publications — well under half of the publication share its burden share would imply under proportionality. Its log2 ABR deepened from −0.95 to −1.20 (ABR 0.52 → 0.43), the only cause below parity in the first period that ended further below it. Laryngeal cancer joined it from above (+0.35 → −0.67). At the other end, thyroid cancer held the pool's largest excess throughout (+3.15 → +3.51; ABR ≈ 11.37 in the last period).

4. Geography converged in direction for most causes, not in level

The distance between the DALY-weighted burden centroid and the first-author research centroid fell for six of the seven burden-tier causes (−14% to −80% between the first and last periods). Last-period distances remain large in absolute terms — 1,081 to 5,957 km: the research centre of every cause still sits on a different part of the map from its burden centre. The one exception is nasopharyngeal cancer: its research was always concentrated near the East-Asian burden centre (780 km in the first period) and moved away, to 1,601 km (+105%). A counterfactual decomposition (each side held fixed in turn) attributes most of the contraction to research-side movement for five causes; the burden side is material for other pharyngeal cancer, and the nasopharyngeal divergence arises mainly from the burden side and the interaction of the two movements, not from research movement alone. Burden centroids moved little in net terms (254–1,049 km first-to-last) against 1,026–7,794 km for research centroids; both the nasopharyngeal divergence and the other-pharyngeal contraction hold under the primary centroid estimator only and are estimator-dependent. Detail on the Geography page.

5. The authorship base broadened

The low- and middle-income country (LMIC) share of first authors across the pooled areas rose from 7.8% in 1995–99 to 40.6% in 2020–24. China accounts for much of the rise (2.7% → 23.8% of all parsed records) but not all of it: excluding China, the LMIC share of all parsed records still rose from 5.1% to 16.8%.

6. Typology: one robust technology-ignited type, one stable misalignment, four unclassified

Under the rule-based decoupling typology, otitis media is resolution-dilution (a relative share dilution only — annualised absolute DALYs rose for all seven causes). Oral cancer is technology-ignited, its assignment unchanged under all three threshold sets. Hearing loss is typed constant on dynamics only (under all three sets), and must be read together with its level — ABR 0.43 in 2020–24, the pool's deepest deficit: a stable misalignment, not an absence of one. Nasopharyngeal, laryngeal, other pharyngeal and thyroid cancer match no rule under the main thresholds and are reported as unclassified, not forced into a type. Detail on the Typology page.

CRS/polyps. Formulation note: this area is reported under three formulations, which count three different literatures. Under the corrected primary formulation its pool share rose (+26.3%); under the original formulation it fell (−26.6%); under a pre-frozen backup formulation built only from modern post-consensus terms it rose +251.0%. No share-direction claim for this area is carried as a headline conclusion. The CRS share direction is therefore not counted among the headline findings above.

The preregistered expectations, scored

Expectation (as registered)VerdictWhat the data show
(1) Classic infectious diseases will fall in publication share but not in absolute output. Fully matched Otitis media is the textbook case: share 9.2% → 3.3%; output 2,229 → 3,706 publications per period.
(2) Burden is shifting towards lower-income regions while research geography does not follow. Largely not supported Research geography did follow: centroid distances contracted for six of the seven causes, and the LMIC first-author share rose 7.8% → 40.6%. The expectation survives only in its level form — the remaining distances are still 1,081–5,957 km — and one cause's research geography (nasopharyngeal cancer) moved away from its burden.
(3) Share gains will concentrate in diseases marked by treatment or technology shifts. Half matched The head-and-neck cancers fit the expectation. Obstructive sleep apnoea, the clearest technology-associated area, rose once and then plateaued, its yearly share sitting at roughly 13–15% in every year since 2005. The CRS point originally attached to this expectation is now reported under three formulations (caveat above), not as evidence about the biologics era either way.

A scorecard of one full, one largely failed and one half is what a preregistered descriptive study should expect to publish; the failed expectation (2) is the more informative result, because it does not support the simplest version of the "research ignores the shifting burden" narrative for this specialty.
