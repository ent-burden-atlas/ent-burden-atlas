Geography: burden and research centroids

For each burden-tier cause and period, two centres are computed over the same set of country centres. The burden centroid is weighted by GBD 2023 DALY numbers — it sits where the cause's burden sits. The research centroid is weighted by parsed first-author records — it sits where the cause's publishing authors are. Both use the arithmetic mean centre (the weighted arithmetic mean of country-centre latitudes combined with the circular mean of longitudes; centroid freeze v2), and the distance between them is the haversine great-circle distance. The estimator's diagnostic history, and the side-by-side comparison with the two estimators it replaced, are documented on the Methods page and in the frozen estimator-sensitivity table on the Downloads page.

The distance fell for six of the seven causes (−14% to −80%). Three paths — otitis media, hearing loss and laryngeal cancer — fall monotonically. The others do not: oral cancer's path is a sawtooth (1,214 / 1,558 / 618 / 1,187 km across the middle periods); thyroid cancer rose slightly in the final step (1,674 → 1,756 km, +82 km) after a steep fall; other pharyngeal cancer rose before it fell, ending only 14% below its start. The one exception is nasopharyngeal cancer: its research centroid started close to the East-Asian burden centre (780 km) and moved away period by period to 1,601 km (+105%) — the convergence and rebound seen before the formulation correction were artefacts of non-cancer nasopharyngeal records. Convergence in direction is not convergence in level: last-period distances remain 1,081–5,957 km, so the research centre of every cause still sits on a different part of the map from its burden centre.

Who moved? Separating the two sides in net terms: each cause's burden centroid moved 254–1,049 km first-to-last, while its research centroid moved 1,026–7,794 km. A counterfactual decomposition (each side held fixed in turn) attributes five causes' contractions mainly to research-side movement — where publishing authors are, against a burden geography that moved little. Two causes are exceptions: for other pharyngeal cancer the burden side is material (burden-only +871 km offsetting nearly half of the research-only −1,854 km), and the nasopharyngeal divergence arises mainly from the burden side and the interaction of the two movements (research-only −95 km), so no one-sided attribution is made. The nasopharyngeal divergence and the other-pharyngeal contraction hold under the primary centroid estimator only and are estimator-dependent.
