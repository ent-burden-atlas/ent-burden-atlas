Geography: burden and research centroids

For each burden-tier cause and period, two centres are computed over the same set of country centres. The burden centroid is weighted by GBD 2023 DALY numbers — it sits where the cause's burden sits. The research centroid is weighted by parsed first-author records — it sits where the cause's publishing authors are. Both use the arithmetic mean centre (the weighted arithmetic mean of country-centre latitudes combined with the circular mean of longitudes; centroid freeze v2), and the distance between them is the haversine great-circle distance. The estimator's diagnostic history, and the side-by-side comparison with the two estimators it replaced, are documented on the Methods page and in the frozen estimator-sensitivity table on the Downloads page.

The distance fell for all seven causes (−39% to −80%). Three paths — otitis media, hearing loss and laryngeal cancer — fall monotonically. The others do not: oral cancer's path is a sawtooth (1,048 / 1,369 / 692 / 1,292 km across the middle periods); nasopharyngeal cancer fell to 978 km in 2015–19 and rebounded to 2,467 km in the last period, the largest single-step reversal in this layer; thyroid cancer rose slightly in the final step (1,674 → 1,756 km, +82 km) after a steep fall. Convergence in direction is not convergence in level: last-period distances remain 1,102–5,242 km, so the research centre of every cause still sits on a different part of the map from its burden centre.

All three shares use all parsed records as the denominator, so the excluding-China column is the frozen basis reported throughout this site (it is not renormalised to non-China records). China alone accounts for much of the pooled rise (2.5% → 21.9% of all parsed records), but the excluding-China share still more than tripled (5.4% → 17.9%).
