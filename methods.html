Methods

Design and preregistration

This is a descriptive, denominator-type census. Two denominators are fixed first — a 14-area publication pool and, inside it, a seven-cause burden pool — and every quantity is a composition within them. The protocol, disease pool and analysis layers were fixed in a signed work report and preregistered on the Open Science Framework before record-level production began (registration DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/NUC4B). Two amendments are documented: the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation retired GBD 2021, so Amendment v1.1 substituted GBD 2023 (burden years 1995–2023); and Amendment v1.2 (21 November 2026) corrected five search formulations (see "Formulation correction" below). The study measures the configuration of research allocation only; it makes no clinical-value judgement and no claim about how much research any disease deserves.

Formulation correction (Amendment v1.2, 21 November 2026)

Correction note. A pre-submission audit found that two of the originally frozen formulations used anatomy terms alone, with no cancer limiter — "nasopharyngeal" and "oropharyngeal/hypopharyngeal" — and so counted large amounts of non-cancer literature. A sampled positive-predictive-value (PPV) audit of the original formulations (60 records per stratum per area, classified under rules written before sampling) gave, for nasopharyngeal cancer, PPV 0.333 (95% CI 0.227–0.459) in 2020–2021 and 0.700 in other years; for other pharyngeal cancer, 0.683 and 0.833. The 2020–2021 nasopharyngeal spike was predominantly COVID-19-era nasopharyngeal sampling literature, not nasopharyngeal cancer research. Five formulations were therefore corrected under one principle — a site concept combined with a cancer limiter set, or cancer-specific phrases only: nasopharyngeal and other pharyngeal cancer gained the limiter; oral cancer gained the lip expressions its GBD cause name requires (+487 records over 30 years, previously counted nowhere); laryngeal cancer gained twelve cancer-limited expressions it had been missing (+1,946 records, +24.9% — an undercount in the opposite direction); and CRS/polyps dropped the standalone term "sinusitis", through which 8,934 predominantly acute or general sinusitis records — a different entity — had entered a chronic-disease area. The remaining nine formulations were checked term by term and left unchanged. The census was recomputed in full on the corrected corpus, and every value on this site is on the corrected basis; the original formulations and their outputs are retained in the archive as the query-basis sensitivity. The original frozen results were not deleted or overwritten.

Publication census (L1)

Publications were counted in Europe PMC under one frozen title/abstract query per area, per year, 1995–2024 (420 area–year cells), and aggregated into six five-year periods. The primary basis is the corrected formulation set of Amendment v1.2 (five formulations corrected, nine checked and unchanged; each formulation is listed in the query file and mapping table on the Downloads page). The L1 counts use the Europe PMC hitCount basis (357,712 records). Record-level retrieval for the geographic layer delivered 357,711 area-assigned records, corresponding to 329,250 unique publications (24,998 assigned to two or more areas); retrieval for the five corrected areas was repeated on 21 November 2026 (30 parts, 98,542 records, retrievable equal to hitCount in every part), and the nine unchanged areas retain their 9 November 2026 retrieval. One part contained one "phantom" record — counted in the hitCount but never delivered (D-2: obstructive sleep apnoea 2015–2019, 11,604 vs 11,603). A second phantom in the original retrieval (D-1: oral cancer 2010–2014) did not recur in the corrected re-retrieval. Each phantom was accepted only after three scripted checks passed (unique identifiers; stable hitCount on re-query; identical identifier sets from an independent second retrieval channel), and both are itemised in a frozen discrepancy record shipped with the archive.

Country parsing (L3 production)

A first-author affiliation was present for 325,206 records (90.91%). Affiliation strings were parsed to a country or region by a four-layer dictionary procedure (institution and city dictionaries with disambiguation rules; no imputation), succeeding for 310,722 records (95.55% of those with an affiliation) across 157 countries or regions. A 90-record manual audit detected no errors (0 of 90; 95% upper bound for the error rate ≈ 3.3%). The most frequent first-author countries among parsed records were the United States (69,049 records), China (47,959) and Japan (16,947). Author country is a property of the publishing institution, not of the study population.

Burden data (L2 inputs)

GBD 2023 DALYs (primary) and prevalence (sensitivity calibre), all ages, both sexes, global aggregate plus 204 countries and territories (205 locations), annually 1995–2023, for cause ids 329, 674, 423, 444, 447, 450 and 480. Country DALY sums reproduce 99.83–99.96% of the corresponding global totals. The DALY Number metric is primary; the all-ages rate in the downloads is not age-standardised and is not used. Last-period pairing: burden periods match publication periods for the first five periods; in the last period the burden side covers 2020–2023 against publications 2020–2024. This one-year asymmetry is disclosed wherever last-period burden-relative quantities appear, and a sensitivity analysis restricts last-period publications to 2020–2023.

Attention–burden ratio (L2)

For period t, ABR = (a cause's share of the seven-cause publication pool) ÷ (its share of the seven-cause DALY pool). ABR 1 is proportionality within the pool; log2 ABR is reported, so +1 is a twofold publication excess over burden share and −1 a twofold deficit. Both shares are compositions inside the same seven-cause pool: the ABR is a within-specialty relative measure and supports no comparison outside the pool.

Geographic layer (L3 analysis)

Double-centroid distance. The burden centroid is the DALY-weighted mean centre of country centres (frozen snapshot of the mledoze/countries dataset); the research centroid is the same construction weighted by parsed first-author records. The primary estimator is the arithmetic mean centre: the weighted arithmetic mean of latitudes combined with the circular (unit-vector) mean of longitudes; distances are haversine great-circle distances (R = 6,371.0088 km). The estimator was chosen after a documented diagnostic episode: the chordal (unit-vector) mean, initially frozen, placed several research centroids at implausible Arctic latitudes (oral cancer 1995–99 at 82.0°N) because first-author distributions ring the Pacific and their horizontal components cancel; the spherical geometric median avoided the pole but degenerated for nasopharyngeal cancer, both centroids snapping to the same country centre and the distance collapsing to 0.0–0.3 km for three consecutive periods. All three estimators are frozen side by side for all 42 cause–period cells in the estimator-sensitivity table shipped with the archive. For the nine areas whose retrieval was not repeated, all three estimators agree on the direction of every first-to-last distance change; the five corrected areas were recomputed under the primary (arithmetic mean centre) estimator only, and their corrected distances rest on that estimator.

Author shares. The LMIC share is the proportion of parsed records whose first author is affiliated in a low- or middle-income country (World Bank low-, lower-middle- and upper-middle-income groups combined), with a decomposition excluding China. Income groups are the World Bank classification as frozen on 12 November 2026, held fixed across all periods — a disclosed simplification, since countries' classifications changed over 30 years. For burden-tier causes, the share of first authors in that cause's ten highest-DALY countries is also reported.

Decoupling typology (L4)

Each burden-tier cause is classified from three signals comparing the first and last periods: S, the relative change in its share of the 14-area pool; B, the relative change in its share of the seven-cause DALY pool; and A, the change in log2 ABR. The signed protocol fixed the type names and definitions — resolution-dilution, technology-ignited, burden-tracking, constant — and anything matching no rule is reported as unclassified, never forced. The protocol did not fix numeric thresholds; these were operationalised at the typology stage, before the classification was run (main ±25% for S and B, ±0.5 for A; sensitivity sets ±15%/±0.3 and ±35%/±0.7 are reported with every assignment). The typology is therefore a descriptive classification under pre-stated definitions, not preregistered inference. Two reading rules apply: B is a share, so "burden falls" means relative dilution inside the pool only (annualised absolute DALYs rose for all seven causes, +3.9% to +101.3%); and type names are shape labels, not mechanism claims.

Sensitivity programme and verdicts

  • Lag window. Pairing last-period publications with burden 0, 5 or 10 years earlier changed no cause's last-period log2 ABR sign (7/7 identical across lags). Across the full per-period grid, one cell is not sign-stable: laryngeal cancer in 2005–2009 sits at +0.17 / +0.09 / −0.02 across the three lags — at parity under every pairing — and is disclosed as such rather than absorbed into a blanket stability claim.
  • Publication window. Restricting last-period publications to 2020–2023 moved each last-period log2 ABR by less than 0.05, with no sign change.
  • Burden calibre. The prevalence calibre agrees with the primary calibre on five of seven last-period signs and six of seven trend directions. The sign exceptions are laryngeal cancer and otitis media (hearing loss is 92.8% of the seven-cause prevalence pool, which compresses the other shares towards zero and inflates their ratios); the trend exception is other pharyngeal cancer alone, near-flat in both calibres. The headline level claims hold in kind in both calibres: hearing loss is the most negative value under prevalence, and the cancer excesses are larger, not smaller.
  • Terminology drift. For each area, one backup query was specified and frozen in writing before any backup count was retrieved. The corrected primary and backup formulations agree on share direction for 13 of the 15 rows (14 areas plus the five-cancer combination). The two disagreements are cochlear implants (primary +1.8% vs backup −9.7%) and tinnitus (+6.4% vs −5.8%, a pure denominator effect). CRS/polyps is reported under three formulations (corrected primary +26.3%; original −26.6%; backup +251.0%): the backup formulation uses only modern terms that entered routine use after the 2003–2004 consensus terminology, so it is structurally blind to the sinusitis-era literature. The three formulations count three different literatures, and under the protocol's two-formulation rule no CRS share-direction claim is carried as a headline conclusion.
  • Multi-assignment bases. The record-level quantities were recomputed under fractional weights (each publication contributing 1/k to each of its k areas) and on the single-assignment subset. All seven causes keep their last-period ABR signs under all three bases; otitis media's single-assignment value is +0.22 on the corrected corpus (it was −0.23 on the original corpus).
  • Historical income classification. Reclassifying each period by the World Bank classification in force at its first year raises the LMIC series to 10.5% → 41.7% (fixed classification: 7.8% → 40.6%), and the excluding-China series to 7.8% → 17.9% (fixed: 5.1% → 16.8%). The early-period level is classification-dependent; the rise is not.
  • Query basis. Every primary quantity was recomputed under the original (broad) formulations and set against the corrected basis. The nasopharyngeal share surge disappears (+61.6% original vs +10.6% corrected); its last-period ABR falls from 5.94 to 2.98; its typology assignment changes from technology-ignited under all threshold sets to unclassified under all sets; and its centroid distance changes sign, from a 48% contraction with a final-period rebound to a 105% widening from a close start. Laryngeal cancer corrects in the opposite direction (first-period log2 ABR −0.18 → +0.35). Every headline not involving the corrected areas is unchanged in direction on both bases.

The lag-window and terminology analyses were completed on 12 November 2026, after the primary layers were frozen; the backup queries were fixed before their counts existed. The multi-assignment and historical-income analyses were completed on 13 November 2026 at the revision stage, and the query-basis analysis on 21 November 2026 under Amendment v1.2. They are reported as completions of the protocol's sensitivity programme at their respective stages, not as elements of the original preregistration timestamp.

Use of large language models

Large language models were used as assistive tools in this project: for development and debugging of analysis code, for orchestration of the analysis pipeline, and for assistance in drafting the manuscript. All data were produced by deterministic scripts from the public sources named above; every number reported here was verified against the frozen result files; no data, result or figure was generated by a language model.
